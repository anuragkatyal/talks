The Activities Were Memorable. The Formulas Were Not.

Paper folding, three binomial formulas, and the distance between a lesson that goes well and a lesson that works.

Anurag Katyal Intermediate Algebra Spring 2026
Fifteen minutes. The talk is about three activities I built, why they looked like they worked, and what I found afterwards in my own source code.
THE ASSUMPTION

A physical model makes a symbolic identity unforgetable.

State the bet plainly, because the rest of the talk is about how the bet failed. Nobody in this room disagrees with the premise, which is the problem.
The lessons

Three activities, one sheet of paper each

01

A Tale of Two Squares

x² − y² = (x + y)(x − y)

Fold, tear, and rearrange one square into an (x + y) by (x − y) rectangle.

02

Square-ception

(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²

Partition a square into four regions and compute the corner region two ways.

03

Square+ception

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²

Partition a square of side a + b into four regions and add the regions up.

Thirty seconds each. All three are Doenet activities for Intermediate Algebra, all three use a sheet of paper, all three end with a formula and a practice set.
Activity 01

Difference of squares

x² − y² = (x + y)(x − y)

  1. Start with a square of side x. Fold off a strip of width y.
  2. Tear the strip away; the sheet is now x by (x − y).
  3. Rotate the strip and set it below the sheet.
  4. The result is one rectangle, (x + y) by (x − y), of area x² − y².

Letters on the paper from step one.

images/activity-01.png

Walk the fold quickly. The key point is that this one is fully symbolic: x and y are on the paper from the first step.
Activity 02

Square of a difference

(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²

  1. Split a square of side a into four regions.
  2. Label every missing dimension: a − b, or b.
  3. Region 1 is (a − b)².
  4. Region 1 is also a² − (a − b)b − (a − b)b − b².
  5. Set the two expressions equal.

The subtractive case needs a warm-up about carpets before it works.

images/activity-02.png

No folding here. It is a partition argument with a warm-up about carpet areas, because the subtractive case needs it.
Activity 03

Square of a sum

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²

  1. Start with a square of side a + b. Its area is (a + b)².
  2. Split it into four regions and label every edge a or b.
  3. Region 1 is a². Regions 1 and 2 are a² + ab. Add region 3, then region 4.
  4. The four regions total a² + 2ab + b².
  5. Same square, two expressions. Set them equal.

The additive case: the whole is the sum of its parts.

images/activity-03.png

This is the cleanest of the three, and the one whose formula came back wrong most often. Every running total in step three was picked from a list of seven options.

Part two

Five mechanisms, all of them in my own source code

I went back to the DoenetML looking for a story about student cognition. What I found was a set of design decisions I had made without noticing.

Pivot. This part of the talk is about my design rather than about students. Everything ahead comes from reading my own source code.
Mechanism 01

The activity wrote the algebra; students recognized it

Square+ception, Step 3

What is the area of region 1 and 2?

a² ab b² a² + ab a² + ab + ab a² + ab + b² a² + ab + ab + b²

The same seven options appear in all four questions, and each answer is the previous answer plus one term. The whole derivation can be walked by pattern, without writing anything.

In two of the three activities, no student ever wrote a² + 2ab + b². They found it in a list. The assessment asks them to produce it.

This is the strongest of the five. Read the option list out loud and let the room notice that the answer to each question is the previous one plus a term.
Mechanism 02

The activities were error-suppressing by design

hide="not $firstDim.creditAchieved=1"

In Square+ception this attribute wraps whole sections, not single questions. The next step of the derivation does not exist on the page until the previous one is right.

So nobody ever carried a wrong answer forward to the point where it broke. The productive energy in the room was, in part, an artifact of a design that never let anyone be stuck and wrong in public.

All three activities use this. In the sum activity it hides entire sections, so the lesson is a waterfall: you cannot see step three while step two is wrong.
Mechanism 03

The misconception appeared only as a distractor

Square+ception, Step 3, item 5

What does a² + ab + ab + b² simplify to?

a² + a²b² + b² a² + 2ab + b² a² + 2a²b² + b² a² + b² (a² + b²)²

a² + b² is on that list. It is the error the lesson was built to prevent, and the student meets it exactly once, as an option to pass over, next to the right answer.

Rejecting a misconception from a menu is not the same as not producing it a week later. I had treated the two as equivalent.

Point at a squared plus b squared in the option list. That is the error on the assessment, and this is the only time in the lesson a student is asked to think about it.
Mechanism 04

The practice trained filling, not choosing

Square+ception

Square-ception

Every practice input arrived prefilled with the right template, in a set where only one formula was in play. Students who could fill the boxes were never asked which formula applied. On the assessment the boxes are gone and the three formulas are mixed together.

Each activity's practice set contains only that activity's formula, and the template is already in the box. Selection is the skill the test measures and the one the practice removed.

Three activities, one architecture

Students got three passes at the same scaffold: partition, label, total, equate, formula, prefilled practice. What three lessons made fluent was the scaffold. The assessment is not in that genre.

Also flag the asymmetry: the subtractive case needs a warm-up on subtracting overlapping areas and got the same time and structure as the additive one.
Revisions

What I would change

01

Ask for the expanded form in writing, before any options appear. Recognition can come afterwards; it cannot come first.

02

Let a wrong answer run. Replace gating with a consequence the student can see and then repair.

03

Have students expand (a + b)² themselves and compare it with a² + b², instead of rejecting the misconception from a menu once.

04

Retire the prefilled templates in the final practice set and mix all three formulas, so selection is required.

05

Give the subtractive case more room than the additive one. They are the same lesson only if you ignore what subtraction of overlapping regions costs.

—

And, across all five: measure a week later, and measure selection rather than execution.

One change per mechanism. Say which one you would do first and why, and admit the last one costs class time you may not have.
In closing

The activity was a demonstration. It was not an argument.

Engagement, clarity, and correctness were all measured while the model was still on the desk. The formula was assessed after it left. Nothing in the design carried the student from one to the other, and I had assumed the paper would do it.
